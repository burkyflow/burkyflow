Database reactivation reconnects a business with eligible past customers and dormant leads already in its CRM. Relevant follow-up and booking assistance identify people who still want a service. Results depend on list quality, timing, customer interest, and the offer.

## Which contacts should you reactivate?

Start with customers due for maintenance or prospects who requested an estimate. Review contact eligibility and communication preferences. Remove opted-out, invalid, and duplicate contacts before launching a campaign.

## How does CRM reactivation work?

1. Segment eligible contacts by last service date, service type, or enquiry stage.
2. Send a short message explaining why you are contacting them and what they can do next.
3. Route replies to your team or booking workflow, with a clear path to a person.
4. Stop campaign messages when someone books, declines, or opts out.
5. Log outcomes in your CRM so future campaigns use current information.

## Does reactivation require new ad spend?

You can contact eligible people already in your database without buying new advertising. Messaging, implementation, platform usage, and ongoing operation can still have costs. Compare those costs with completed jobs attributable to the campaign.

## How should you measure a campaign?

Track reachable contacts, delivered messages, interested replies, booked appointments, completed jobs, and opt-outs. A comparison with a similar uncontacted group can help estimate incremental results.

## What makes a good first campaign?

Choose one segment and one relevant service. Agree on the booking definition, timing, human handoff rules, and reporting before expanding.
