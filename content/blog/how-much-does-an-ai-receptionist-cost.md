"How much does an AI receptionist cost?" is the first question most owners ask, and the honest answer is "it depends on what you ask it to do." That is not a dodge. An AI receptionist that answers and takes messages is a different product from one that qualifies callers, books into your dispatch board, updates your CRM, and transfers emergencies to the on-call technician. This post explains how the pricing is built, what moves it, and how to compare it with the alternatives.

## How AI receptionist pricing is structured

Most providers, including us, price in three parts:

1. **Setup fee.** One-time. Covers writing the call script from your rules, connecting your phone number, calendar, and CRM, testing real call scenarios, and training your team on the handoff.
2. **Monthly fee.** Covers running the service: hosting, monitoring, updates to the script as your rules change, and support.
3. **Usage.** Some providers bill per minute or per call above an included amount. Ask whether usage is included, capped, or billed separately, and what the rate is.

A quote that only shows one of these is hiding the other two.

## The five factors that move the price

**Call volume.** More calls mean more minutes and more usage. A shop that gets twenty calls a day pays less than a practice that gets two hundred.

**What the receptionist does.** Answering and messaging is the base. Qualification, booking, CRM updates, payment links, and transfers each add configuration and integration work. The more the receptionist completes on the call, the more it costs to set up, and the more it saves after.

**Locations and numbers.** Each phone line, location, or department with its own rules adds setup. Multi-location routing is a different tier from a single number.

**Integrations.** Connecting a calendar is simple. Connecting field service software, a dealership DMS, or a practice management system takes more work and may need a certified integration.

**Hours and languages.** Around-the-clock coverage is standard. Additional languages and custom after-hours rules add scope.

## How to compare it with the alternatives

Put all three options on the same scale, cost per booked job:

- **A full-time receptionist** costs a salary, benefits, and cover for breaks, sick days, and the hours the office is closed. One person, one shift.
- **A live answering service** charges per call or per minute and takes messages for you to return. Cheap per call, but the message still has to be turned into a booking by someone, later.
- **An AI receptionist** costs a setup fee and a monthly fee, works every shift, handles simultaneous calls, and books the job on the call.

Divide each option's monthly cost by the jobs it actually books, including the after-hours and overflow calls the first two options miss, and the comparison usually settles itself.

## Questions that get you an accurate quote

Bring these answers to any pricing conversation:

- How many calls do you get per week, and how many go unanswered? Your phone system or call logs show this.
- Which calls should the receptionist book, and which should reach a person?
- What calendar, dispatch board, or CRM should bookings go into?
- How many numbers and locations?
- Do you need Spanish or another language?

With those, a provider can give you a real number instead of a range.

## What a fair quote looks like

A fair quote states the setup fee, the monthly fee, what usage is included, and what each extra costs. It also says who maintains the receptionist after launch and how fast they respond when something breaks. If the quote is just "from X per month," ask what X excludes.
