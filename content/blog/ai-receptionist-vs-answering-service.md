When calls go unanswered, most service businesses look at two options: a live answering service staffed by people, or an AI receptionist that answers with a trained voice agent. Both pick up the phone. They differ in what happens after hello, and that difference decides which one actually brings in work.

## What a live answering service does

A live answering service routes your overflow and after-hours calls to a team of operators. They usually work from a script you provide: greet the caller, take a name, number, and reason for calling, and pass the message to you by text or email. Some services can book into a shared calendar or transfer urgent calls to an on-call number.

The operators handle many businesses at once, so they know your script, not your business. Anything outside the script usually becomes a message for you to return.

## What an AI receptionist does

An [AI receptionist](/services/ai-voice-receptionist) is a voice agent set up with your services, service area, hours, pricing rules, and booking rules. It answers every call at once, without hold queues, and can:

- Ask your qualifying questions and tell a real job from a price shopper
- Check calendar availability and book the appointment during the call
- Send the caller a text confirmation
- Write the call summary and details into your CRM
- Transfer to a person when the call meets the handoff rules you set

The key difference is that the call ends with a booking or a clear next step, not a message waiting for someone to call back.

## Where an answering service is the better fit

A live service still makes sense in some cases:

- **Calls that need human judgment.** Sensitive conversations, complaints, or situations where the caller expects to talk to a person.
- **Very low call volume.** If you get a handful of calls a week, a simple message service may be all you need.
- **No booking system.** If appointments are set by hand and there is no shared calendar, an AI agent has nothing to book into until that is in place.

## Where an AI receptionist is the better fit

An AI receptionist tends to win when:

- **Calls come in at the same time.** Several callers at once never wait in a queue.
- **Speed decides who gets the job.** A caller who needs a plumber today will often call the next business if nobody books them now.
- **You want the details in your CRM.** Every call is logged with a summary, so follow-up does not depend on someone retyping a message.
- **After-hours calls matter.** Nights, weekends, and holidays are covered without overtime or extra staff.
- **Your rules change.** Updating prices, service areas, or booking rules is a settings change, not retraining a team.

## How pricing works for each

Answering services usually charge by the minute or by the call, often with a monthly plan that includes a set amount and overage charges beyond it. Your bill rises with call volume and call length.

AI receptionists are usually priced as a setup fee plus a monthly fee, sometimes with usage charges for very high volume. The cost depends more on how much the agent does (booking, CRM updates, multiple locations) than on how long each call lasts.

To compare them fairly, do not compare the monthly bills alone. Compare the cost per booked job:

**Cost per booked job = monthly cost ÷ jobs booked from answered calls**

A service that only takes messages may cost less each month but book fewer jobs, because some callers move on before anyone returns the call.

## Using both

Many businesses do not need to pick one. A common setup is to let the AI receptionist answer every call, book routine jobs, and answer common questions, then transfer anything that needs a person to your team or to a live service. You decide the transfer rules, such as emergencies, complaints, or calls from existing customers.

## Questions to ask before you choose

1. How many calls do you miss each week, and at what times? Your phone system or call logs will show this.
2. What share of your calls could be booked straight away with the right information?
3. Do you have a calendar or field service tool that bookings can go into?
4. Which calls must always reach a person?
5. Where do you want call details to end up: your CRM, a text, an email?

Your answers will usually point clearly to one option or a mix of both.
