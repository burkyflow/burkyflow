An AI answering service answers your business phone with a trained voice agent instead of a person or a voicemail greeting. The good ones do far more than take a message: they ask your qualifying questions, book the appointment against your real calendar, text the caller a confirmation, and write the details into your CRM. For a small business that misses calls when the team is busy or the office is closed, that is the difference between a lead and a lost one.

## What an AI answering service actually does

Think of it as a receptionist who never puts anyone on hold:

- **Answers on the first ring, every hour of every day.** Nights, weekends, holidays, and the lunch rush are all covered.
- **Handles several calls at once.** Three callers at 8 a.m. on a Monday never hear a busy tone.
- **Asks your questions.** Name, what they need, where they are, how urgent it is, whichever details you decide matter.
- **Books the job.** It checks your calendar or dispatch board and offers real times, then confirms by text.
- **Transfers when needed.** Calls that match your rules, such as emergencies or existing customers with a complaint, go to a person.
- **Writes everything down.** Every call becomes a record in your CRM with a summary, so follow-up does not depend on memory.

The phrase "answering service" used to mean a call center that took messages. An [AI answering service](/services/ai-voice-receptionist) ends the call with a booking or a clear next step instead.

## How it works behind the scenes

1. **Setup.** You provide your services, service area, hours, pricing rules, booking rules, and the situations that must reach a person. This becomes the agent's script and its limits.
2. **Phone connection.** Calls forward to the agent on overflow, after hours, or on a dedicated line. Your existing number stays.
3. **The call.** The agent greets the caller in your business's tone, asks the qualifying questions, and books or transfers.
4. **After the call.** The caller gets a text confirmation. Your team gets the summary in the CRM or dispatch board. Missed calls that somehow still reach voicemail get a missed call text back offering to book.
5. **Review.** You read call summaries and adjust the rules. The agent improves as the rules do.

Setup for a single line typically takes a week or two. A multi-location setup with custom routing takes longer.

## What it costs

Pricing is usually a one-time setup fee plus a monthly fee. The monthly fee depends on:

- **Call volume.** More calls, more minutes.
- **What the agent does.** Answering and messaging costs less than answering, qualifying, booking, and updating the CRM.
- **Locations and numbers.** Each line or location adds configuration.
- **Integrations.** Connecting to a calendar, a CRM, or dispatch software is part of setup.

Compare it with the alternatives honestly. A live answering service charges per call or per minute and takes messages. A full-time receptionist costs a salary and benefits and works one shift. An AI answering service costs a fraction of a salary and works every shift. The right comparison is cost per booked job, not the monthly bill.

## How to tell a good one from a message-taker

Ask any provider these questions:

- Does it book into my actual calendar, or just say someone will call back?
- Can it transfer a live call to a person, and under what rules?
- Where do the call details go? If the answer is "an email," that is a message-taker.
- What happens when it does not understand the caller?
- Can I hear a recording of a real call it handled?
- Who sets it up and keeps it working after launch?

A provider that cannot answer the last question is selling software, not a service.

## Who it suits

Any business that lives on the phone and cannot answer every call: HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and roofing companies; dental and medical practices; auto repair shops; law firms; real estate teams; staffing firms. The common thread is that the caller wants to book something, and the business loses the job if nobody picks up.
