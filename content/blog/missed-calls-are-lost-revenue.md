Every unanswered call from a new customer is potential lost revenue. Missed-call recovery means responding to those inquiries, qualifying the caller, and helping them book a service. An [AI voice receptionist](/services/ai-voice-receptionist) handles overflow and after-hours calls using your service information, booking rules, and human escalation instructions.

## Why it happens

Crews are on jobs, the front desk is busy, and after hours nobody may be available. Some callers leave a message; others contact another business. Your call logs show how often this happens.

## How do you calculate lost revenue from missed calls?

Start with unique, qualified inquiries. Exclude spam, repeat dialing, and calls about existing bookings. Then estimate:

**Potential recovered revenue = qualified missed inquiries × recovery-to-booking rate × average completed job value.**

For example, 40 qualified missed inquiries, a hypothetical 25% recovery-to-booking rate, and a $300 average completed job value imply $3,000 in potential revenue. These figures illustrate the calculation; they are not client results or industry benchmarks. Adjust for cancellations and uncompleted jobs before comparing revenue with system costs.

## What to do about it

1. Review call logs and establish your answer rate, booking rate, and response time.
2. Decide whether the AI handles overflow, after-hours calls, or a dedicated line.
3. Define qualification questions, service areas, calendar availability, and human handoff rules.
4. Connect summaries and bookings to your [CRM and lead pipeline](/services/crm-lead-systems).
5. Test urgent calls, unavailable slots, and out-of-scope requests before launch.
6. Review completed bookings and call quality, then adjust the rules.

## Does AI call answering replace the front desk?

It supports your team by covering repeat questions and unattended calls. Calls needing judgment or exceptions should follow the human handoff rules agreed before launch.

## What should you measure after launch?

Track qualified inquiries, booked appointments, completed jobs, and revenue attributed to recovered inquiries. Completed work is a better basis for financial return than bookings alone.
