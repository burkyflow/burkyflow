Service business owners ask a version of this question every week: "I don't just need calls answered. I need the follow-up done. Do I want an AI receptionist or an AI agent?" The two terms get used loosely by vendors, so the honest answer starts with what each one actually does.

## What an AI receptionist does

An [AI receptionist](/services/ai-voice-receptionist) lives on the phone. It answers inbound calls, asks your qualifying questions, books appointments against your calendar, transfers calls that need a person, and logs the call in your CRM. Its job is the conversation that happens when the phone rings.

What it does not do on its own: chase a lead who did not book, remind a customer about a quote, or text a past customer who is overdue for service. Those happen after the call, or without a call at all.

## What an AI agent does

"AI agent" is a broader term. In a service business it usually means automation that watches your data and acts on it: a new lead arrives and gets a text within a minute; a quote sits unanswered for three days and gets a follow-up; a customer's maintenance is due and they get a booking link; a reply comes in and gets routed to the right person. An agent can also make outbound calls, read replies, and decide the next step based on rules you set.

Its job is everything around the call: the follow-up, the reminders, the reactivation, the handoffs. It runs on your [CRM and workflow automation](/services/crm-lead-systems), not on the phone line.

## The difference in one sentence

The AI receptionist handles the conversation when a customer reaches out. The AI agent makes sure the conversation happens, and keeps it going afterwards.

## Which one you need

Ask where your leads are leaking:

- **Calls go unanswered.** Nights, weekends, busy hours, voicemail. You need the receptionist first. No amount of follow-up fixes a call nobody picked up.
- **Calls are answered but leads go cold.** Quotes not chased, web forms not called back for hours, past customers never contacted. You need the agent first. The phone is fine; the follow-up is missing.
- **Both.** Most service businesses, once they look at the numbers. Leads come in by phone, by form, and by text; some are answered, some are not; almost none are followed up consistently.

## Why they work best together

Separately, each one has a gap. Together, they close the loop:

1. The receptionist answers the call and books the job, or logs the lead if the caller did not book.
2. The agent sees the unbooked lead and follows up by text within minutes, then again on a schedule.
3. The caller replies. The agent books the appointment, or hands the reply to a person if it needs judgment.
4. After the job, the agent schedules the next reminder, the review request, and the reactivation message months later.
5. When that reactivation message produces a call, the receptionist answers it, and the loop starts again.

The receptionist is the front door. The agent is everything that happens after someone walks through it, and the reason they come back.

## Questions to ask a vendor

- Does your AI receptionist write the call into my CRM, or just send me an email?
- When a caller does not book, what follows up, and how soon?
- Can the follow-up read replies and book, or does it only send?
- Who decides the rules for when a person takes over?
- Can I see the follow-up messages before they go out?

A vendor who sells only the phone part will leave you to build the follow-up. A vendor who sells only the automation will leave your calls on voicemail. Ask for both, running on the same data.
