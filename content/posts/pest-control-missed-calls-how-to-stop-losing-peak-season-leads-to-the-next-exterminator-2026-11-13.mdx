Pest control is a phone-first business with a timing problem. The calls arrive in waves: termite swarms in spring, ants and wasps in early summer, mosquitoes through July, and rodents moving indoors once the weather turns. Those waves land exactly when your technicians are in attics, crawlspaces and backyards, and when your office is busiest. A caller who has just found mice in the pantry or a wasp nest by the front door does not leave a voicemail and wait. They call the next company on Google, and whoever answers first usually gets the job.

The good news is that most of these lost calls are recoverable with a few simple systems. You do not need more office staff. You need every caller to get a fast, useful response, even when nobody can pick up.

### Why Pest Control Loses More Calls Than Most Trades

Three things make missed calls especially expensive in pest control:

- **Seasonal spikes.** Call volume in peak months can be two to three times the slow season, but most companies staff the office for an average week.
- **Small, mobile teams.** Many owner-operators run routes themselves. A technician wearing a respirator under a house cannot answer a ringing phone.
- **High lifetime value.** The first call is rarely a one-time job. A homeowner who starts with a single treatment often converts to a quarterly or monthly service plan worth hundreds of dollars a year, renewed for years.

Here is a simple example. Say your business gets 300 inbound calls a month in peak season and misses 20% of them. That is 60 missed calls. If half are real new-customer inquiries and you would normally book half of those, you are losing about 15 new customers a month. If a third of them would have joined a $45-per-month service plan, that is 5 plans, or about $2,700 a year in recurring revenue lost every peak month, before counting the one-time treatments. Plug in your own numbers from your phone system's call log.

### Step 1: Find Out When and Why Calls Are Missed

Pull the last 90 days of call logs and sort missed calls by hour and day of the week. Most pest control companies find the same pattern: missed calls cluster between 11 a.m. and 3 p.m., when everyone is on a route, and after 5 p.m., when homeowners get home and notice the problem. Also note how many missed callers never called back. Those are the leads you are actually losing.

### Step 2: Send a Missed-Call Text-Back Within a Minute

The fastest fix is an automatic text to anyone whose call goes unanswered. In GoHighLevel and most business phone systems, you can trigger it the moment a call is missed. Keep it short, human and specific to your trade:

"Hi, this is Dana with Greenline Pest Control. Sorry we missed your call, our techs are out on routes. What kind of pest are you dealing with, and what's your address? We can usually get someone out within 48 hours."

Asking for the pest type and address turns the text into the start of a booking, not just an apology. Most people will reply to a text long before they would leave a voicemail. Include your business name in the first message and honor STOP replies automatically so you stay compliant with US texting rules.

### Step 3: Cover After-Hours and Overflow Calls

Evening and weekend calls are often your most urgent ones: a bat in the bedroom, bed bugs found on a Sunday, or wasps near a child's play set. Decide how you will handle them:

- **Overflow during the day:** forward unanswered calls to a backup line, a [live answering service or an AI receptionist](/blog/ai-receptionist-vs-answering-service) that can collect details and book an inspection.
- **After hours:** route true emergencies, such as stinging insects near people or wildlife inside the home, to an on-call technician, and book everything else into the next available slot.
- **Weekends in peak season:** at minimum, make sure the text-back and online booking are running so no caller hears only a voicemail greeting.

### Step 4: Make Booking the Next Step, Not a Callback

Every missed-call response should end with a booked appointment or a clear time when someone will call. Give callers a link to book an inspection directly into your calendar, and let your office confirm details later. Collect the essentials up front: pest type, where it was seen, whether children or pets are in the home, and whether the address is in your service area. Your technician arrives prepared, and the office avoids a round of phone tag.

### Step 5: Track Recovered Calls Every Month

Measure a handful of numbers so you know the system is paying for itself:

- Missed-call rate by month and by hour of the day.
- Text-back reply rate and how many replies turned into bookings.
- New service plans sold from recovered calls.
- Average time from missed call to booked appointment.

Review them before each peak season. If missed calls still spike at the same hours, adjust staffing or overflow coverage for those windows. For a deeper look at the math behind this, see [how to calculate lost revenue from missed calls](/blog/missed-calls-are-lost-revenue).

### Key Takeaways

*   **Peak season is when you miss the most calls:** Spring and summer demand lands while your team is in the field, so plan coverage for those months specifically.
*   **Text back in under a minute:** A short, pest-specific text that asks for the problem and the address turns a missed call into a booking conversation.
*   **Measure recovered plans, not just calls:** The real value of a recovered pest control lead is the recurring service plan that often follows the first visit.
