The anniversary of a vehicle purchase is a date every dealer and lender has on file, and almost none of them use. It is not a sales event. It is a check-in: a once-a-year message that reviews where the customer stands and offers whatever is genuinely useful at that point. Done consistently, it keeps the customer in the relationship through the years when nothing else would.

## Why the anniversary works

- **It is expected.** A yearly review feels like a service, not a pitch. Banks and insurers do it.
- **It is predictable for the customer.** They learn that the dealership checks in once a year, and they open the message.
- **It is a natural moment for numbers.** Twelve more payments made, a year more depreciation, a year of credit history.
- **It compounds.** Year one, two, three, four. Each check-in adds to the record.

## What to say each year

The content changes as the loan ages:

**Year one.** Credit has improved for many customers, especially subprime buyers who paid on time. The check-in offers a rate review: "You've made 12 on-time payments. You may qualify for a lower payment. Want us to check?" It also confirms service history and offers the first service contract re-offer if factory coverage is ending.

**Year two.** Equity is forming. The check-in reports it: "Your [Year Model] is now worth about what you owe. That opens options: refinance, or a newer vehicle at a similar payment." Lease customers are 12 months from maturity; the check-in starts that conversation early.

**Year three.** Positive equity for most customers, factory warranty ending for many, and the vehicle entering the age where repair costs climb. The check-in offers the three-way choice: keep and cover with a service contract, refinance, or replace.

**Year four and beyond.** Replacement window. The check-in leads with a trade estimate and a replacement payment, while still offering a service contract for customers who want to keep the car.

Each message is short, specific, and ends with one reply option. The customer chooses.

## Making it automatic

The anniversary is a date field, so the trigger is trivial. The content depends on the customer's current numbers, which is where the data layer matters:

1. On the anniversary, the engine pulls payoff, value, rate, payment history, warranty status, and lease status.
2. It selects the message variant for the year and the customer's position.
3. It sends with the consent check and opt-out applied.
4. Replies route to the finance desk with the numbers attached; an [AI receptionist](/services/ai-voice-receptionist) can take the call and book the review.
5. The outcome writes back, and the next anniversary is already scheduled.

A [CRM connected to the loan and DMS data](/services/crm-lead-systems) does all of this without a calendar reminder for anyone.

## Keeping it a check-in, not a campaign

Rules that protect the tone:

- One message per anniversary. No sequence.
- Lead with the customer's position, not the store's inventory.
- Offer "stay as you are" as a real option.
- Suppress if the customer was contacted by another trigger in the last 30 days.
- A person answers every reply.

## What to measure

- Anniversaries contacted per month
- Reply rate by year of ownership
- Reviews completed, and outcomes: refinance, service contract, trade, no change
- Retention: share of customers still financed or serviced with you at each anniversary
- Deals originating from check-ins versus fresh leads

The retention curve by anniversary is the chart to watch over time.

## Compliance

Consent and opt-out on every message. Equity and payment statements must be estimates described as such. No pressure language. A check-in that reads as a sales push loses its one advantage.
