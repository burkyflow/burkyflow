When the inputs come from the business's own data, the return on automated follow-up does not need a sales pitch. It needs a calculator. The same is true of the rules that govern it: the TCPA and, for collections, the FDCPA are not obstacles to work around. They are requirements that, built into the system from the start, make the program safe to scale. This post covers both, because a return that depends on breaking the rules is not a return.

## The ROI math

For each funnel, five numbers:

1. **Triggers per month.** From the DMS, CRM, or loan data: warranty expirations, declined products, equity crossovers, lease maturities, declined estimates.
2. **Contactable share.** Customers with consent and a valid number.
3. **Response and conversion rates.** Reply rate, booking rate, and sale rate. Use conservative figures from a pilot, not a vendor's best case.
4. **Gross per outcome.** Product gross, service RO average, deal gross, from your own records.
5. **Program cost.** Subscription, usage, and management, monthly.

Revenue = triggers × contactable share × conversion rate × gross per outcome. Return = revenue minus program cost. Run it per funnel, then add the funnels, because they share the cost.

The result is usually persuasive on its own, and it is the business's number, not the vendor's. A [CRM with reporting built in](/services/crm-lead-systems) keeps it updated monthly against actuals.

## Why conservative inputs win

A pilot on one funnel for 90 days gives real rates. Using those instead of industry claims produces a smaller projection that the owner believes, and that the program then beats. Overpromising is how automation vendors lose renewals; underpromising with real data is how they keep them.

## The TCPA guardrails

The Telephone Consumer Protection Act governs texts and calls to consumers. The rules that must be in the system, not in a training manual:

- **Prior express consent** for texts and for calls made with automated systems. For marketing messages, prior express written consent. Capture it at the point of sale or on the web form, store it with the customer record, and check it before every send.
- **Opt-out on every message**, honored immediately and everywhere. "STOP" must work, and the suppression must apply across all funnels.
- **Calling and texting hours** within the permitted window in the customer's time zone.
- **Identification.** Every message says who it is from.
- **Reassigned numbers.** Check numbers against reassignment data where required, so a message does not reach a stranger.
- **Record keeping.** Consent, message content, timestamps, and opt-outs retained.

The dispatch layer enforces all of this on every message from every funnel. No template bypasses it.

## The FDCPA guardrails

The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act governs communications about debts in collection, and state laws add to it. Where any automated messaging touches delinquent accounts:

- **Separate the paths.** Servicing reminders before a due date and marketing messages are not collection communications. Once an account is delinquent, messaging moves to a collections path with its own rules.
- **Content and frequency limits.** What can be said, how often, and through which channels are regulated. Templates are written with counsel.
- **Disclosures.** Required statements in collection communications.
- **No marketing to accounts in collection.** Suppress them from every marketing funnel.
- **Dispute and cease handling.** A customer's dispute or request to stop must be routed to a person and honored.

## Putting them together

The ROI case and the compliance case are the same system described from two sides:

- Consent capture increases the contactable share, which increases revenue.
- Suppression rules reduce wasted messages, which reduces cost and complaints.
- Separate servicing and collections paths keep the lender safe and the customer's trust intact.
- Audit trails make every number on the ROI report defensible.

A program that cannot show its consent records has no ROI, because one complaint can erase it.

## What to measure

- Projected versus actual revenue and cost, per funnel, monthly
- Consent coverage: share of customers with valid consent on file
- Opt-outs per thousand messages
- Messages blocked by compliance rules
- Complaints or inquiries (target zero)

## A note on scope

This post describes general principles, not legal advice. TCPA and FDCPA obligations depend on the business, the message, and the state. Have counsel review the consent language, the templates, and the collections path before anything sends.
