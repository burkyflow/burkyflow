A BDC agent sends a follow-up: "Have you had a chance to look at the financing options we sent?" The customer replies: "No, I haven't." The agent logs it as not ready and schedules another touch in two weeks. That is the single most expensive mistake in follow-up, because "no, I haven't" is not a cold reply. It is the hottest lead in the queue.

## What "no, I haven't" actually means

Read the reply for what it says:

- The customer is still interested enough to answer.
- They have not bought elsewhere, or they would have said so.
- They have not reviewed the options, which means nothing has changed their mind.
- The thing stopping them is time, not a decision.

Compare the alternatives. "Yes, and we went with someone else" is lost. "Yes, and we're still thinking" is a customer with objections you have not heard. "No, I haven't" is a customer with no objections yet, who just needs the next step made easy. Treating that as a two-week pause hands them to whoever calls next.

## The problem is the question

"Have you had a chance to look?" invites a yes/no answer about the past. Most people answer honestly, say no, and feel slightly guilty. The conversation stalls.

Reframe the question so that any answer moves forward:

- Instead of "Have you had a chance to review?" ask "Would it help if I walked you through the two options in five minutes, or would you rather I send the one that fits your budget?"
- Instead of "Are you still interested?" ask "Are you still looking at the SUV, or has something changed?"
- Instead of "Did you get my message?" ask "Which day this week works for a quick call, Tuesday or Thursday?"

Each version assumes the customer is still in, and asks them to choose a next step rather than confess to not having taken one.

## Route "no, I haven't" to a person, now

The right response to "no, I haven't" is a person within minutes, not another automated message in two weeks. The workflow:

1. **Detect the reply.** Text and email replies are read by the system. A reply that signals "not yet" rather than "not interested" triggers a priority task.
2. **Alert a human with context.** The agent sees the vehicle, the financing options sent, and the full thread, and calls or texts while the customer is still looking at their phone.
3. **Offer the shortcut.** "I can do this in five minutes right now" removes the time objection that caused the "no."
4. **Book the next step in the conversation.** An appointment, a call, or a single document to sign.

This is where a [CRM with automated routing](/services/crm-lead-systems) earns its keep: not by sending more messages, but by making sure the right replies reach a person fast. An [AI voice receptionist](/services/ai-voice-receptionist) can even make the first callback, confirm the customer has a few minutes, and transfer to the agent.

## Sort replies by what they mean

Not every reply deserves the same response. A simple classification helps:

- **"No, I haven't"** or "not yet": hot. Person within minutes.
- **"Still thinking"**: warm. Ask what is holding them back, then route to a person.
- **"Went elsewhere"**: closed. Thank them, ask for service business later.
- **"Stop"**: opt out immediately, no exceptions.

Automation handles the sorting. People handle the hot and warm ones.

## What to measure

- Share of replies that are "not yet" versus "not interested"
- Time from a "not yet" reply to human contact
- Appointments set from "not yet" replies
- Deals closed from leads that said "no, I haven't" at least once

Most dealerships find that the "not yet" group converts better than fresh internet leads, because the customer already engaged twice.

## The reframe, in one line

Stop asking customers whether they have done something. Ask them which of two easy next steps they want, and when a customer says they have not had time, give them your time instead of another reminder.
