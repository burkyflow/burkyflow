Most service businesses spend real money to earn the chance to quote a job: the ad click, the first conversation, the drive out, the hour on site, and the time it takes to write up the numbers. Then the estimate goes out by email, and the follow-up depends on whether someone remembers to make it. Usually nobody does. The customer gets busy, a competitor calls back first, and a job you were well placed to win quietly goes to someone else.

An estimate follow-up sequence in your CRM fixes that without adding staff. Every open quote gets a planned series of short, useful touches that stop the moment the customer says yes, says no, or asks a question.

### Why Unanswered Estimates Are Your Cheapest Revenue

An open estimate is the warmest lead you have. The customer asked for a price, let you into their home or business, and has a real problem with a real budget. Compare that with a brand-new lead from an ad, where you still have to earn the first conversation.

Run the numbers for your own business. Say you send 40 estimates a month with an average job value of $4,500 and you close 30% of them. That is 12 jobs and $54,000. If consistent follow-up moves your close rate to 36%, that is about 2 more jobs, or roughly $10,800 a month, from quotes you already did the work to produce. Your numbers will differ, but it is worth doing this math before you spend another dollar on ads.

### Set Up an Estimate Pipeline in GoHighLevel

Follow-up only works if every quote is tracked in one place. In GoHighLevel, or any CRM with pipelines, create a pipeline just for estimates with stages like these:

1. **Estimate requested:** a site visit or consultation is booked.
2. **Estimate sent:** the quote is delivered and the follow-up sequence starts.
3. **Engaged:** the customer replied, asked a question, or requested a change.
4. **Won:** a deposit is paid or the contract is signed.
5. **Lost:** the customer declined, chose someone else, or stayed silent through the full sequence.

Record the estimate amount as the opportunity value and the service type as a custom field. That gives you a live view of open quote dollars and lets you tailor messages by job type. If your estimating tool (Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, or QuickBooks) can send a webhook or connect through Zapier, use it to move the opportunity into "Estimate sent" automatically so nobody has to remember.

### A 5-Touch Follow-Up Sequence That Doesn't Feel Pushy

The goal is to be helpful and easy to say yes to, not to nag. This sequence works well for most home service and local trades businesses:

- **Day 0 (within an hour of sending):** A text such as "Hi Maria, it's Jake from Summit Roofing. I just emailed your estimate for the roof repair. Happy to walk through any line item, just reply here."
- **Day 2:** An email that answers the most common question for that service. For HVAC that may be financing options; for roofing, warranty terms. Include a photo from a similar finished job.
- **Day 5:** A text with a simple question: "Did the estimate cover everything you needed, or should we adjust the scope?" Questions get more replies than reminders.
- **Day 10:** A call task for the estimator or office manager, created automatically in the CRM with the quote amount and site notes attached.
- **Day 21:** A final text: "We'll close out your estimate for now. If the timing changes, reply here and we'll pick it right back up." This polite close-out often earns more replies than any other message in the sequence.

Any reply, booked appointment, or move to Won or Lost should end the sequence immediately. Nothing damages trust faster than an automated "just checking in" after someone has already paid a deposit.

### Match the Follow-Up to Job Size and Urgency

Not every quote deserves the same treatment. Use the opportunity value to branch the workflow:

- **Small jobs (under $1,000):** Fully automated texts and emails, with a link to book online.
- **Mid-size jobs ($1,000 to $10,000):** Automated touches plus one call task for the office.
- **Large jobs (over $10,000):** Automated touches plus a personal call from the estimator or owner, and a prompt to offer a revised option if the customer hesitates on price.

Urgency matters too. A quote for a failed water heater needs same-day follow-up, while a kitchen remodel can breathe for a week. Set timing by service type instead of using one schedule for everything.

### Keep Text Follow-Up Compliant

In the US, business texting is covered by the TCPA and by carrier rules for A2P 10DLC registration. Only text people who gave you their number for this purpose, register your business texting number, name your company in the first message, and honor STOP replies automatically. GoHighLevel tracks opt-outs at the contact level, but confirm your workflow respects each contact's do-not-disturb settings before you launch.

### Measure What the Sequence Is Worth

Track a handful of numbers every month:

- Estimates sent and total open quote value.
- Close rate and average days from estimate to signed job.
- Replies by touch, so you know which messages to keep and which to rewrite.
- Lost reasons: price, timing, chose another company, or no response.

Compare the three months before launch with the three months after. If "no response" was your biggest lost reason before and shrinks afterward, the sequence is doing its job. Lost quotes are not wasted either. Add them to a [database reactivation](/services/database-reactivation) campaign 6 to 12 months later, when budgets and timing may have changed.

### Key Takeaways

*   **Treat open estimates as your warmest leads:** They already cost you time and money to produce, so a small lift in close rate often pays more than new ad spend.
*   **Track every quote in a CRM pipeline:** An estimate pipeline with job values and service types makes follow-up automatic and visible.
*   **Use a short, helpful sequence that stops on reply:** Five well-spaced touches across text, email, and a call task win more jobs without annoying customers.
