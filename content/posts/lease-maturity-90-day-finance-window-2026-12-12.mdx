A lease has something almost no other customer relationship has: a known end date. Ninety days before that date, the customer has to decide what to do with the car, and every option is a finance transaction. Return and lease again, buy the car out, finance the buyout, or walk away. The dealership or lender that gets to the customer first, with the right option, usually wins the next three years. Most wait until the lender's return notice has already gone out.

## The options, and who wins each one

At maturity the customer can:

1. **Return and lease new.** The best outcome for the originating dealer, if the dealer starts the conversation early.
2. **Buy the car out.** Often a good deal for the customer when the residual is below market value. If you do not offer the buyout financing, their bank will.
3. **Finance the buyout.** A loan on a car you know the full history of.
4. **Return and go elsewhere.** The outcome when nobody reached them.
5. **Extend the lease.** A short-term option that keeps the customer in your system.

Each of these is a different message, and the right one depends on three numbers you already have: the residual value, the current market value, and the customer's mileage.

## The 90-day timeline

Work backwards from maturity:

- **Day 120 to 90.** Check equity: is the market value above the residual? If yes, the customer has equity in a lease, which can be applied to a new vehicle or captured in a buyout. Send the first message: "Your lease ends on [date]. Here's what your options look like." Specific, not generic.
- **Day 90 to 60.** Book the lease-end inspection or appointment. This is where the conversation about the next vehicle, or the buyout, actually happens.
- **Day 60 to 30.** Handle paperwork: new lease, buyout financing, or extension. Confirm the return date and condition expectations for returning customers.
- **Day 30 to 0.** Logistics and loose ends. No new offers at this point; the decision should already be made.

The customers who get a message at day 20 have usually already decided, and not in your favor.

## Building the trigger

Lease maturity is the easiest lifecycle trigger to automate because the date is fixed at signing:

**Store the maturity date and mileage allowance** in the CRM at contract time, not in a spreadsheet someone checks quarterly.

**Recalculate equity monthly.** Residual value against a current market estimate, adjusted for mileage. Flag customers in positive equity first.

**Fire the day-120 message automatically.** A short text or email with the end date and an invitation to review options. Replies route to a finance manager or BDC agent with the contract details attached.

**Track the decision.** Each lease moves through states: contacted, appointment set, decided (new lease, buyout, extension, return), completed. A [CRM built for this](/services/crm-lead-systems) shows at a glance which maturities are unworked.

## The messages that work

Lease-end customers respond to specifics:

> Hi [Name], your [Year Model] lease ends on [date]. Based on current values you may have equity that could lower your next payment. Want a quick review of your options? Reply YES.

> Hi [Name], your lease ends on [date]. If you'd like to keep the car, we can arrange the buyout and financing in one visit. Reply YES for the numbers.

One message, one option, one reply. Replies that wait more than a few minutes for a response lose value fast, so an [AI receptionist](/services/ai-voice-receptionist) or a dedicated agent should pick them up immediately.

## What to measure

- Leases maturing per month, and share contacted by day 90
- Appointment rate from the first message
- Retention: share of maturities that end in a new lease, buyout, or extension with you
- Buyout loans originated
- Equity captured on returned vehicles

Retention is the headline number. A store that retains a large share of its maturing leases has built a recurring customer base that costs nothing to acquire.

## Compliance

Lease-end messages are financial communications: consent for texting, an opt-out in every message, and accurate statements about equity ("may have equity" based on an estimate). Keep the lender's own return process and notices intact; your outreach adds to them, it does not replace them.
