A homeowner with a leaking water heater used to type "plumber near me" into Google and scroll through ads, map pins and ten blue links. More and more, that same homeowner now asks a question instead: "Who is the best-rated plumber in Plano that can come out today?" The answer comes back from ChatGPT, Perplexity or a Google AI Overview as a short list of two or three businesses, with ratings, a sentence about each and a button to call.

If your business is not in that short list, you are not on page two. You are simply not in the conversation. Answer engine optimization (AEO), sometimes called generative engine optimization (GEO), is the work of making sure AI search tools know your business, trust it and recommend it.

### Why "Ask ChatGPT" Is Becoming the New "Search Google"

Google now shows AI Overviews above the regular results for many searches, and ChatGPT and Perplexity answer local questions with maps and business listings. For a customer, that is faster: one answer instead of ten tabs. For a local business, it changes the competition. Instead of fighting for one of ten spots on a results page, you are competing for one of a handful of names an AI tool is willing to stand behind.

The upside is that most local competitors have not done anything about this yet. The businesses that put the basics in place now are the ones answer engines will learn to cite.

### How AI Answer Engines Decide Which Business to Recommend

AI search tools do not pick businesses at random. They pull from the same public information people use, then favor businesses where that information is clear, consistent and backed by other sources. In practice, they look for:

- **Consistent business facts** across your website, Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp and industry directories.
- **Clear answers on your own site**: what you do, where you work, what it costs and how fast you can come.
- **Reviews and ratings** that are recent, plentiful and mention the specific services people ask about.
- **Mentions on trusted sites** such as local news, chambers of commerce, supplier directories and community pages.

The five steps below cover each of those signals.

### Step 1: Make Your Business Details Match Everywhere

Start with your name, address, phone number, hours and service area. Search for your business on Google, Bing, Apple Maps, Yelp, the Better Business Bureau and the directories in your trade, such as Angi or Houzz. Fix every listing that uses an old phone number, a different spelling of your name or the wrong hours.

This sounds basic, but conflicting details are one of the most common reasons an AI tool hesitates to recommend a business. If two sources disagree about where you are or when you are open, the safer choice for the AI is a competitor whose details agree.

### Step 2: Turn Your Service Pages Into Clear Answers

AI tools quote pages that answer questions directly. Review each main service page and make sure it answers the questions customers actually ask:

- What exactly is included in this service?
- Which cities, neighborhoods or ZIP codes do you cover?
- What is the typical price range, and what changes it?
- How quickly can you schedule a visit, and do you offer emergency service?

Write the answers in plain sentences near the top of the page, not hidden in a PDF or an image. A short FAQ section at the bottom of each service page, written in the words customers use, gives answer engines ready-made passages to cite.

### Step 3: Add Structured Data Search Engines Can Read

Structured data, also called schema markup, is code on your website that labels your information so machines can read it without guessing. For most local service businesses, three types matter most:

- **LocalBusiness** (or a more specific type such as Plumber or HVACBusiness) for your name, address, phone, hours and service area.
- **Service** for each service you offer and the area you serve.
- **FAQPage** for the question-and-answer sections from Step 2.

Your web developer or website platform can add this in an afternoon. Test it with Google's Rich Results Test to confirm it is valid.

### Step 4: Earn Reviews and Mentions AI Tools Trust

Reviews are one of the strongest signals for any local recommendation. Ask every happy customer for a review, and make it easy with a direct link sent by text right after the job. Encourage customers to mention the service and their town: "fixed our tankless water heater in Frisco the same day" tells an AI tool far more than "great service."

Beyond reviews, look for places your business can be mentioned by others: local sponsorships, supplier "find an installer" pages, trade association directories and local news features. Each credible mention is another source confirming that you exist and do good work.

### Step 5: Check What AI Tools Say About You Today

Before changing anything, find out where you stand. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google the questions your customers ask, such as "best roof repair company in [your city]" or "emergency electrician near [your neighborhood]." Note which businesses come up, what is said about each, and whether any details about your business are wrong.

Repeat the same questions once a month and keep a simple log. Changes take time to show up, but tracking the same questions month after month shows whether you are moving into the answers your customers see.

### Key Takeaways

*   **AI answers are the new top of the results page:** When customers ask ChatGPT or Google's AI for a local pro, only a few businesses get named, so being one of them matters more than ranking tenth.
*   **Consistency and clarity win:** Matching business details everywhere and plain-language answers on your service pages make your business easy for AI tools to trust.
*   **Reviews and mentions are proof:** Specific, recent reviews and mentions on credible local sites give answer engines the confidence to recommend you.
