The words matter more than most people in the business realize. A warranty is a promise included in the price of the product by its maker or seller. A vehicle service contract is a separate agreement, sold for a separate price, that pays for covered repairs. Federal and state rules treat them differently, regulators pay attention to the distinction, and the robocall era made "extended warranty" a phrase customers associate with fraud. Every message a dealership or lender sends has to hold the line: service contract, not warranty.

## Why the distinction exists

- **A warranty** comes with the vehicle. The manufacturer's factory warranty, or a dealer's own warranty on a used car, is part of the sale and is governed by warranty law.
- **A service contract** is an optional product, usually administered by a third party, with its own price, term, deductible, and cancellation rights. It is regulated as a service contract or, in some states, as an insurance product.

Calling a service contract an "extended warranty" misdescribes what the customer is buying. It can trigger advertising rules, consumer protection claims, and state service-contract statutes. It also sounds like the scam call.

## Where the wrong word creeps in

- Text and email templates written quickly.
- AI receptionist scripts that use the customer's own phrase back to them.
- Website pages and landing pages copied from older material.
- Sales conversations, where "warranty" is simply the word people use.
- Dashboards and internal reports, which then leak into customer-facing copy.

Automation makes this worse if the template is wrong, because the wrong word goes out thousands of times. It makes it better if the template is right, for the same reason.

## The rules for every message

1. **Name the product correctly.** "Vehicle service contract" or "service contract." If the customer says "warranty," the reply can acknowledge and correct: "The factory warranty ends on [date]; what we offer after that is a vehicle service contract."
2. **Identify who offers it.** The dealership presents it; the administrator or provider is named in the contract and should be named in detailed materials.
3. **State what it covers, plainly.** Covered components, term, deductible, and exclusions in summary, with the full contract available.
4. **State the price and how it is paid.** Lump sum or added to financing, with the effect on the payment if financed.
5. **State cancellation rights.** Service contracts are generally cancellable, with a refund schedule.
6. **No urgency tricks.** No "final notice," no implied prior contact, no pretending to be the manufacturer.

## The trust dividend

Customers who receive a message that says "vehicle service contract from the dealership that services your car" react differently from customers who receive "extended warranty" from an unknown number. Getting the words right is a compliance requirement, and it is also the reason the message gets read.

## What to measure

- Templates in use, and share reviewed and locked
- Messages blocked by the phrase check
- Complaints or regulator inquiries referencing product language (the target is zero)
- Cancellation rate within 60 days, as a signal of whether the product was described accurately
