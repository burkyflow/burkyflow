Two numbers describe every financed vehicle: what the customer still owes, and what the car is worth. The difference between them is the equity gap, and it moves every month as the balance drops and the market shifts. A dealership or lender that watches that gap for every customer has a trigger that fires at exactly the moment a conversation becomes worth having. Most do not watch it, so the moment passes.

## Why the gap matters

The equity position tells you what the customer can do:

- **Deep negative equity.** Not much today. Keep the customer informed and set a date to re-check.
- **Near zero.** The customer is about to have options. This is the moment to start the conversation.
- **Positive equity.** The customer can trade without rolling anything over, refinance into a better position, or use the equity as a down payment on a newer vehicle at a similar payment.
- **Large positive equity.** A strong trade candidate, and a vehicle you want for your used lot.

The same customer passes through all four states over the life of the loan. The trigger is the transition, not the state.

## Building the number

You need three inputs for every financed vehicle:

1. **Current payoff.** From the loan system, or estimated from the amortization schedule using the original terms and payments made.
2. **Current value.** From a valuation feed, adjusted for mileage. Mileage can be estimated from service visits or asked for in a quick message.
3. **Threshold rules.** What counts as "workable" for your store: for example, equity above a set amount, or negative equity below a set amount with 12 or more months of on-time payments.

Recalculate monthly. Flag every customer who crosses a threshold since the last run. That list is your outreach for the month.

## Turning the trigger into a message

Each transition has a natural message:

**Crossing into positive equity.** "Your [Year Model] is now worth more than you owe. That equity could lower your payment on a newer vehicle, or we can show you a refinance. Want the numbers? Reply YES."

**Negative equity shrinking into range.** "Your loan has reached a point where we may be able to improve your position. Want a quick review?"

**Large equity plus an in-demand model.** "We're looking for vehicles like your [Year Model] for our used inventory. Yours may be worth more than you think. Want an offer?"

One message, one reason, one reply. The reply reaches a finance manager or acquisition specialist with the payoff, value, and gap already on screen.

## Where the data lives

The hard part is not the math. It is that payoff sits in the loan system, the vehicle in the DMS, service mileage in the service system, and the customer in the CRM. A [connected CRM](/services/crm-lead-systems) pulls the three numbers together, runs the comparison, and writes the result back as a field the marketing rules can use. Once that exists, the trigger runs itself.

## Combining with other triggers

The equity gap is stronger in combination:

- **Equity plus a high original rate** points to a refinance.
- **Equity plus a large repair estimate** points to a trade.
- **Equity plus lease maturity** points to a new lease with the equity applied.
- **Equity plus a service visit** means the customer is on site; the advisor can mention the offer at write-up.

Build the equity field first. The combinations follow.

## What to measure

- Customers crossing each threshold per month
- Contact rate and reply rate per transition type
- Appointments and deals from equity triggers, by outcome (trade, refinance, acquisition)
- Gross per deal from equity-triggered conversations versus walk-in deals

Equity-triggered deals tend to be cleaner: the customer's numbers are known, the vehicle is known, and the conversation starts from facts.

## Compliance

Equity messages must be accurate for the recipient. "May be worth more than you owe" based on an estimate is fine; a guaranteed figure is not. Texting requires consent and an opt-out. Keep the valuation source and the calculation date with each message so the claim can be shown.
