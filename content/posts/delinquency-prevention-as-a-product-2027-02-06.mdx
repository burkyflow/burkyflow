A large share of first missed payments on auto loans are not decisions. They are a due date that fell on the wrong day, a card that expired, a customer who assumed autopay was set up when it was not. By the time the account reaches collections, a fixable problem has become an expensive one for both sides. Delinquency prevention treats the days before a due date as the moment to act, and packages that outreach as a service the customer is glad to have, rather than a warning they resent.

## Prevention versus collection

Collection starts after a payment is missed and is governed by strict rules. Prevention happens before the due date and is a customer service function:

- A reminder a few days before the payment is due.
- A confirmation when it clears.
- An easy way to update a payment method or move a due date.
- An early, friendly check-in if a payment is a day or two late, before any notice goes out.

The tone, the timing, and the system are different from collections, and they should be kept separate.

## The prevention sequence

1. **Welcome.** At funding: the first due date, the amount, how to pay, and how to set up autopay. Many first-payment defaults are prevented here.
2. **Pre-due reminder.** Three to five days before each due date: amount, date, and a one-tap link to pay or confirm autopay.
3. **Payment confirmation.** When it posts, a short thank-you. This builds the habit of reading the messages.
4. **Day-one check-in.** If the payment has not posted the day after the due date: "Looks like your payment hasn't come through yet. Need to update your card or move the date? Reply 1 or 2." A person answers any reply within minutes.
5. **Due-date flexibility.** Where the lender allows, let the customer shift the due date to match payday, once, through the conversation.

Every step is an automated message from the loan data, with a human on every reply. An [AI receptionist](/services/ai-voice-receptionist) can handle the inbound calls these messages generate, take a payment method update where permitted, and route anything sensitive to a person.

## Why it is a product

For the lender, prevention reduces roll rates into delinquency, collection costs, and charge-offs. For the customer, it protects their credit rebuild and removes the stress of remembering. Framed that way, it is a benefit of financing with you, and it can be presented as one at delivery: "We'll remind you before every payment and make it easy to adjust if life changes."

For a dealership with its own lending, or a buy-here-pay-here operation, prevention is also retention: a customer who completes a loan without a missed payment is the best candidate for the next vehicle.

## Building it

- **Loan data feed.** Due dates, amounts, payment posting, and autopay status, daily.
- **Message templates** reviewed by compliance, locked, and versioned.
- **A separate path for delinquent accounts.** Once an account is past a defined point, prevention messages stop and the collections process, with its own rules, takes over.
- **Reply handling.** Payment method updates and due-date changes through secure, permitted channels; distress or dispute signals routed to a person immediately.
- **Reporting.** First-payment default rate, on-time rate, roll rate from current to 30 days, and the share of day-one check-ins that resolved.

A [CRM connected to the servicing system](/services/crm-lead-systems) runs the sequence and keeps the audit trail.

## What to measure

- First-payment default rate before and after the welcome sequence
- On-time payment rate at 6 and 12 months
- Roll rate from current to 30 days past due
- Resolution rate of day-one check-ins
- Collection costs per account

## Compliance

Prevention messages are servicing communications and must still respect consent, opt-out, timing, and content rules. Once an account is delinquent, the FDCPA and state collection laws govern what can be said, by whom, and when, and the messaging must change accordingly. Keep the two systems separate, and have counsel review every template.
