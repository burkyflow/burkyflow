Every automation vendor's dashboard goes up and to the right. Appointments set, leads engaged, messages delivered, all in large green numbers. Dealer principals have seen enough of these to know what happens next: the sales floor says the appointments were not real, the service manager says the bookings did not show, and the dashboard is never opened again. Inflated numbers do not just mislead. They end the relationship.

## How dashboards inflate

The inflation is rarely a lie. It is loose definitions:

- **"Appointment set"** counted when the customer says "maybe Thursday" rather than agreeing to a time.
- **"Lead engaged"** counted on an automated reply the customer never read.
- **"Contact made"** counted on a delivered text, not a two-way exchange.
- **Rebooked no-shows** counted as new appointments.
- **Duplicates** across channels: the same customer counted from the web form, the text, and the call.
- **Attribution grabs**: a customer who walked in on their own counted because the system had sent them a message that month.

Each is defensible in isolation. Together they produce a dashboard nobody believes.

## The counting rules

Honest reporting comes from definitions written down before the first report:

1. **An appointment is a specific date and time the customer confirmed.** Anything less is a "follow-up scheduled."
2. **Contact means a two-way exchange.** A reply, a conversation with the AI receptionist, or a call that connected.
3. **Show means the customer arrived**, as recorded by the service or sales system, not by the automation.
4. **A rescheduled appointment counts once.** A no-show counts as a no-show.
5. **One customer, one count** per outcome, regardless of how many channels touched them.
6. **Attribution requires a path.** The customer replied, booked, or arrived through the message or conversation. Being on a list is not attribution.
7. **Revenue comes from the DMS**, not from estimates in the automation platform.

Write the rules into the dashboard's definitions so anyone can check them.

## Why honest numbers win

A dashboard with smaller, true numbers does three things an inflated one cannot:

- **It survives the floor check.** When the sales manager pulls the appointment list and finds real customers at real times, the numbers gain authority.
- **It makes decisions possible.** A real show rate tells you whether to fix confirmations. A fake one tells you nothing.
- **It earns the renewal.** Dealers renew with vendors whose numbers match their own reports. They quietly drop the ones whose numbers do not.

## Reconcile with the dealership's own data

The strongest proof is a monthly reconciliation: appointments the automation reports against appointments in the CRM and shows in the DMS. Differences are investigated and explained, not hidden. A [CRM built with these definitions](/services/crm-lead-systems) makes the reconciliation a report, not a project.

## What to measure, about the measuring

- Appointments reported versus appointments found in the CRM
- Shows reported versus shows in the DMS
- Duplicate rate (customers counted more than once)
- Share of "contacts" that were two-way

If those reconcile within a small margin every month, the dashboard can be trusted. If they do not, fix the counting before adding features.
