Most database campaigns ask the customer to do something vague: call us, come in, reply for more information. The pre-qualification text asks them to do something concrete that takes two minutes and gives them a number: "See what you qualify for, without affecting your credit." Sent to the right segment at the right time, it produces pre-qualified customers while the store is closed, each one with an estimated tier and a payment range attached.

## The message

Short, specific, and about the customer's situation:

> Hi [Name], this is [Store]. Based on your [Year Model] financing, you may qualify for a lower payment or a newer vehicle at a similar one. Check your options in 2 minutes, no credit score impact: [link]. Reply STOP to opt out.

Three things make it work: it is from a business the customer knows, it names a benefit tied to their own loan, and the action is small and safe.

## The segment

The message is only true for some customers. Send it to them and no one else:

- **Refinance-ready.** Equity in the vehicle and a rate above today's market, with 12 or more clean payments.
- **Lease maturity.** 90 to 120 days from lease end.
- **Equity crossover.** Customers who recently moved into positive equity.
- **Credit rebuild.** Subprime customers at the 12-month mark with on-time history.
- **Aging vehicles.** Customers whose vehicle is at the age where repair costs climb and a replacement at a similar payment is realistic.

Each segment comes from the data layer described earlier in this series. The pre-qualification link is the same for all of them; the reason in the message changes.

## What happens overnight

The customer taps the link, completes a short form, consents to a soft pull, and sees an estimated tier and payment range. The system then:

1. Creates a pre-qualified lead in the CRM with the result attached.
2. Sends a confirmation text: "Here's your estimate. Want to see vehicles that fit, or review a refinance? Reply 1 or 2."
3. Handles the reply: matched inventory for 1, a refinance summary for 2, and an appointment offer either way. An [AI receptionist](/services/ai-voice-receptionist) can run this conversation at 11 p.m. as well as at 11 a.m.
4. Queues a priority task for a finance manager or BDC agent to call in the morning, with the whole thread visible.

By opening time, the pipeline has customers with known tiers, known interests, and in many cases booked appointments.

## The morning follow-up

The morning call is the 5-minute rule applied to the start of the day: the pre-qualified customers are called first, before fresh internet leads, because they have already done the work. The call confirms the appointment or moves the customer to the next step. Anyone who pre-qualified but did not reply gets one text at mid-morning and a re-check in a week.

## Why it fills the pipeline

- The customer's main objection, the credit pull, is removed.
- The result is a number, not a promise.
- The action fits in the time it takes to wait for a coffee.
- The segment is built from true conditions, so the message rings true.

A pre-qualification campaign to a well-built segment typically outperforms the same message with a "reply YES" call to action, because the customer gets value before they have to talk to anyone.

## What to measure

- Messages sent, link taps, form completions
- Pre-qualifications by tier
- Replies and appointments set overnight
- Funded deals from the campaign, by segment
- Opt-outs per thousand sent

Opt-outs are the quality control. A well-targeted segment produces very few.

## Compliance

This is a financial marketing text: prior express consent is required, every message carries an opt-out, and the pre-qualification must be described accurately as a soft inquiry and an estimate, not an offer of credit. Sending hours must respect the rules in each customer's time zone. Build the message once with compliance review and do not let it drift.
