The subscription price of GoHighLevel, or any similar CRM and automation platform, is the number everyone quotes. It is also the smaller part of what a running automation costs. Texts, phone minutes, emails, AI voice and conversation minutes, phone numbers, and some integrations are billed per use. A business that runs the recovery funnels in this series will generate thousands of those actions a month, and the bill reflects it. None of this is a problem. It is a cost of doing business that needs to be understood before it arrives.

## What is billed per use

Exact rates change and vary by region, so check the current platform pricing; the categories are stable:

- **Outbound and inbound texts**, usually per message segment. Long messages cost more than short ones.
- **Phone calls**, per minute, inbound and outbound.
- **Phone numbers**, per number per month.
- **Email**, per message sent, through the platform's sending service.
- **AI conversation and AI voice**, per message or per minute, where the platform's own AI features are used.
- **Workflow premium actions**, where the platform charges for certain steps such as external lookups or AI steps.
- **Carrier registration and compliance fees** for business texting, which are one-time and recurring charges imposed by the carriers.

Each is small. Volume makes them matter.

## Estimating the monthly usage bill

Build the estimate from the funnels you plan to run:

1. **Count the triggers per month.** Service reminders, warranty-end offers, refinance messages, declined-repair follow-ups. Your DMS or field service data gives the counts.
2. **Multiply by messages per trigger.** Usually one outbound plus a reply or two.
3. **Add inbound call minutes** handled by the AI receptionist, from your call logs.
4. **Add numbers and compliance fees.**
5. **Apply current rates** from the platform's pricing page, with a margin for growth.

The result is a usage line that sits beside the subscription. For a store running the full engine it is typically larger than the subscription, and the revenue it produces is larger still.

## Controlling it

Usage costs are controllable by design:

- **Contact budget.** The engine's per-customer message cap limits volume as well as annoyance.
- **Segment quality.** Messages sent only to customers for whom the offer is true produce replies, not waste.
- **Short messages.** One segment where possible.
- **Suppression rules.** No message to a customer who already bought, already booked, or opted out.
- **AI minutes where they earn.** AI voice on inbound calls that would otherwise be missed pays for itself; AI on a conversation a person could have handled in ten seconds may not.
- **Monitoring.** A usage line on the dashboard, by funnel, so a runaway workflow is caught in days, not at the invoice.

## Who pays what

When an agency or automation partner runs the platform for a business, be clear about the structure before the first invoice:

- **Platform subscription:** who holds the account and pays it.
- **Usage charges:** passed through at cost, marked up, or included in a flat fee.
- **Carrier fees:** one-time registration and recurring charges, usually passed through.
- **Build and management fees:** the partner's own charges, separate from platform costs.

A transparent arrangement shows the business all four lines. The next post in this series covers how to explain this to an owner who sees "$10 at a time" on a statement.

## What to measure

- Usage cost by category and by funnel, monthly
- Cost per reply, per booking, and per sale, by funnel
- Usage cost as a share of recovered revenue
- Opt-outs and bounces, which are paid-for messages that produced nothing

A funnel whose cost per booking rises month over month needs its segment or its message fixed.
