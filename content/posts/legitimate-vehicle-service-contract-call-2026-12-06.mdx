Everyone has received the robocall: "We've been trying to reach you about your car's extended warranty." Those calls were illegal, dishonest, and so common that the phrase became a joke. They also did real damage to a legitimate product. A vehicle service contract sold by the dealership that services the car, to a customer whose factory coverage is about to end, is one of the most useful things F&I offers. The challenge is marketing it in a world where the words sound like spam.

## Start with the right words

The product is a **vehicle service contract**, not a warranty. A warranty comes from the manufacturer and is included in the price of the car. A service contract is a separate agreement that pays for covered repairs for a set term and price. The distinction matters legally, and it matters for trust: a message that says "service contract" sounds like what it is, and a message that says "extended warranty" sounds like the robocall.

Every message, script, and web page should use the correct term and say who is offering it.

## What makes the call legitimate

The robocall had four problems: the caller was unknown, the car was unknown, there was no consent, and the product was vague. The legitimate version fixes all four:

1. **The customer knows you.** They bought or service the car at your store. The message comes from a name they recognize.
2. **You know the car.** Year, model, mileage, in-service date, and when the factory coverage ends. The message can be specific.
3. **You have consent.** The customer agreed to texts or calls, and the opt-out is in every message.
4. **The offer is concrete.** Which coverage, for how long, at what price, with what deductible.

A message built on those four points does not sound like a scam, because it is not one.

## When to make the offer

Timing turns a pitch into a service:

- **Factory coverage ending.** The most natural moment. "Your bumper-to-bumper coverage ends in 60 days. Here are your options from the store that services your car."
- **After a covered repair.** A customer who just saw what a repair costs understands the value.
- **At a declined-product follow-up.** The customer said no in F&I at delivery. A year later, with real ownership experience, many say yes.
- **At service visits.** The advisor can mention it when the customer is already thinking about the car's condition.

Each of these is a date or an event in the DMS. A [workflow](/services/workflow-automation) can watch for them and send the message at the right time, instead of blasting the whole database.

## The message itself

Keep it short, specific, and honest:

> Hi [Name], this is [Store] where you service your [Year Model]. Your factory coverage ends on [date]. We offer vehicle service contracts that cover repairs after that. Want the options and pricing? Reply YES or call [number]. Reply STOP to opt out.

No urgency tricks, no "final notice," no pretending a previous contact happened. The customer replies, and a person with the vehicle file explains the coverage.

## Handle replies like a sale, not a campaign

The reply is the moment of value. It should reach a person within minutes, with the vehicle details and coverage options in front of them. If the F&I office is busy, an [AI receptionist](/services/ai-voice-receptionist) can answer the call, confirm the vehicle, and book a time to review options. What it must not do is let the reply wait until tomorrow.

## Measure it separately

Track service contracts sold from post-sale outreach apart from contracts sold at delivery:

- Customers contacted per trigger
- Reply rate and appointment rate
- Contracts sold, by coverage level
- Cancellation rate within the first 60 days (a quality check on how the product was explained)

A low cancellation rate tells you the message and the conversation were honest.

## Compliance checklist

- Use "vehicle service contract," not "warranty."
- Identify the dealership and the product provider.
- Text or call only with consent, with an opt-out in every message.
- State price, term, and deductible accurately.
- Keep records of consent and of each contact.
