Every service business has the same moment: the technician or advisor presents the estimate, and the customer's face changes. The work is needed. The number is more than they expected. "Let me think about it" follows, and the job leaves with the customer. Most of those declines are about cash flow this week, not about the repair. Repair financing at the service desk changes the question from "can I afford this" to "can I afford this per month," and the answer is usually yes.

## Where financing belongs in the conversation

Not after the decline. In the estimate:

1. **The diagnosis and the recommendation.** What is wrong, what it needs, what happens if it waits.
2. **The total.** Clearly, once.
3. **The monthly option.** "Or about X per month over 12 months, with approval in a couple of minutes if you'd like."
4. **The choice.** Pay today, finance, or schedule it.

Customers who hear the monthly figure before they decide are in a different frame of mind from customers who hear it as a rescue after saying no.

## Setting it up

**Choose a program.** Third-party consumer financing for home services and auto repair is widely available, with soft pre-qualification and quick decisions. The shop presents the option; the lender makes the decision and carries the loan.

**Put the payment on every estimate.** The estimate software or the CRM should calculate the monthly figure automatically from the total and the program's standard terms, so the advisor never has to do math on the spot.

**Train the ask.** Advisors and technicians need a script for the monthly option that is short and comfortable. The goal is to offer it every time, not to push it.

**Make applying easy.** A link texted to the customer, or a tablet at the desk. Two minutes, soft pull, result on screen.

## Following up on declines

Even with financing in the estimate, some customers will decline. That decline is now a logged opportunity with a known amount and a known payment:

- **A week later.** One message restating the repair, the monthly figure, and a booking option. "Still want to get the water heater sorted? It's about X per month, and we can fit you in Thursday."
- **At the next lifecycle moment.** If the customer books a different service, the advisor sees the open estimate and raises it.
- **At the natural deadline.** Seasonal items, such as heating before winter, get a reminder as the season approaches.

A [CRM that logs declined estimates](/services/crm-lead-systems) runs these automatically. Replies book through text or through an [AI receptionist](/services/ai-voice-receptionist) that can quote the financed figure and confirm the slot.

## Why it changes the numbers

- **Average ticket rises** when customers choose the full repair over the minimum fix.
- **Close rate rises** when the monthly figure is in the estimate.
- **Declined-work recovery** becomes measurable instead of lost.
- **Cash flow improves** because the lender pays the shop at completion.

## What to measure

- Share of estimates presented with a monthly option
- Financing applications and approval rate
- Close rate on estimates with and without the payment shown
- Declined estimates recovered within 60 days
- Average ticket, financed versus unfinanced

## Compliance

The shop presents the option; it does not make credit decisions or promise approval. Terms come from the lender and must be stated accurately, including any promotional conditions. Follow-up texts need consent and an opt-out. Keep the financing conversation separate from any collection activity on past-due accounts.
