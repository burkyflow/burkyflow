Guaranteed asset protection is explained once, in the finance office, in the last ten minutes of a two-hour delivery, to a customer who has already said yes to a car and no to three other things. A meaningful share decline it. Then nobody mentions it again, even though the loan that made GAP relevant runs for years. Of all the F&I products, GAP is the one most worth a second, better-timed conversation, and the one that almost never gets it.

## What GAP does, plainly

If a financed vehicle is totaled or stolen, the insurer pays its current value. If the loan balance is higher than that value, the customer owes the difference. GAP coverage pays that difference. It matters most when:

- The down payment was small.
- The term is long.
- The vehicle depreciates quickly.
- Negative equity was rolled in from a previous loan.

In those cases the gap can be thousands of dollars, owed on a car that no longer exists.

## Why customers decline at delivery

The reasons are predictable:

- Decision fatigue. GAP comes after the price, the trade, the rate, and the service contract.
- Abstract risk. "Totaled" feels unlikely in a new car.
- Bundled suspicion. It arrives alongside products the customer may not value.
- Price presented monthly alongside everything else, so it looks like one more add-on.

None of those reasons mean the customer does not need GAP. They mean the timing was wrong.

## When to re-market it

The second conversation should happen when the risk is concrete and the customer's attention is free:

1. **30 to 60 days after delivery.** The customer has settled in, the payment is real, and the file shows the exact loan-to-value position. "Based on your loan, if the car were totaled today you could owe about [X] beyond the insurance payout. GAP covers that. Want the details?"
2. **When the equity calculation shows a wide gap.** If you track payoff versus value (and you should), the customers most exposed are easy to find.
3. **At the first insurance renewal.** Customers are thinking about coverage anyway.
4. **After an accident claim in the area, or a customer's own near-miss.** Careful here: relevant, not opportunistic.

The first two are triggers from data you already hold. They can run automatically through a [CRM connected to the loan file](/services/crm-lead-systems).

## The honest version of the offer

GAP is easy to oversell. The re-marketing message should:

- State the customer's actual exposure, from their loan and a value estimate, with the estimate date.
- Give the price and term plainly, and whether it can be added to the loan or paid separately.
- Note that coverage may be available from their own insurer, and let them compare.
- Make it one reply to get details, and one reply to opt out.

A customer who compares and still buys from you trusts you more afterwards. A customer who feels pushed cancels, and GAP cancellations are expensive in chargebacks.

## Who should not be re-marketed

Equally important:

- Customers whose loan-to-value is already comfortable. GAP adds little for them, and offering it anyway damages credibility.
- Customers who already have GAP through their lender or insurer.
- Customers who opted out of marketing.

The trigger rules should exclude them, so every message that goes out is to someone for whom GAP makes sense.

## Handling the reply

The reply needs a person who can quote and enroll, within minutes. If the finance office is busy, an [AI receptionist](/services/ai-voice-receptionist) can confirm the customer's details, explain what GAP covers in plain terms, and book a five-minute call to complete it.

## What to measure

- Customers flagged as GAP-eligible per month
- Contact rate, reply rate, and enrollment rate
- GAP revenue from re-marketing, separate from delivery
- Cancellation rate within 60 days
- Claims paid to customers who enrolled through re-marketing, when you can see them

The last number is the one to be proud of. GAP sold to the right customer at the right moment is a product that pays out when it matters.

## Compliance

GAP is regulated as a debt-cancellation product or as insurance depending on the state. Disclosures, pricing, and cancellation rights must follow the applicable rules, and the message must not misstate coverage. Consent and opt-out apply to every text.
