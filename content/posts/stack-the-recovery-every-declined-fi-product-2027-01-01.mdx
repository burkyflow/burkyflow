A customer at delivery might decline four products in ten minutes. Each decline is logged, if at all, as a line on the menu. Then the customer owns the car for five years, during which every one of those products becomes relevant at a different moment. Re-marketing them one campaign at a time means four separate programs, four sets of messages, and a customer who hears from the dealership too often. Stacking them into one funnel means one customer record, one contact schedule, and each product offered once, when it fits.

## The products and their moments

Each declined product has a natural second chance:

- **Vehicle service contract.** Before factory coverage ends, or after the first out-of-pocket repair.
- **GAP.** 30 to 60 days after delivery, when the loan-to-value is known, and whenever the equity gap is wide.
- **Tire and wheel.** At the first tire rotation, or when the service record shows a tire replacement.
- **Prepaid maintenance.** At the first paid service visit, when the customer sees the cost of an oil change.
- **Appearance protection.** Less time-sensitive; best bundled with a service visit in the first year.
- **Key replacement and dent coverage.** Where offered, at the first service visit.

The point is that the moments differ. One campaign that re-offers everything at 90 days hits every product at the wrong time.

## One funnel, product-specific triggers

The stacked funnel runs on the engine described earlier in this series: one data layer, one trigger evaluator, one dispatch path. The product-specific parts are the trigger rules:

1. **Declined flags from the deal.** Which products were offered and declined at delivery. If the F&I menu does not record declines, start recording them; this is the input.
2. **A trigger per product.** Warranty end minus 90 days for the service contract. Delivery plus 45 days for GAP. First tire rotation for tire and wheel. First paid RO for prepaid maintenance.
3. **A contact budget per customer.** No more than one F&I message per month, and no F&I message within two weeks of a service reminder. The funnel schedules around the budget rather than firing everything that qualifies.
4. **Suppression.** Products already purchased, customers who opted out, and vehicles no longer owned are excluded before anything sends.

## The messages

Each product gets one short message in the same voice:

> Hi [Name], this is [Store]. When you picked up your [Year Model] you passed on tire and wheel coverage. You're in for your first rotation next week; if you'd like to add it, reply YES and we'll have the details ready at your visit.

Specific, tied to the moment, one reply. Replies route to the F&I office, or to an [AI receptionist](/services/ai-voice-receptionist) that can explain the product in plain terms and book a quick call.

## Why stacking beats separate campaigns

- **The customer is contacted less and converts more**, because each message is relevant.
- **Compliance is handled once.** Consent, opt-out, and product terminology live in the dispatch layer, not in four campaigns.
- **Reporting is comparable.** Every product's recovery rate is on the same scorecard, so you can see which second chances work.
- **Adding a product is a rule, not a project.**

## What to measure

- Declined products logged per delivery (the input quality)
- Customers contacted per product trigger
- Reply rate, enrollment rate, and F&I gross recovered by product
- Cancellation rate within 60 days by product
- Contact load: F&I messages per customer per quarter

A rising recovery rate with a flat contact load is the sign the stack is working.

## Compliance

Service contracts are service contracts, not warranties. GAP is a debt-cancellation or insurance product depending on the state, with its own disclosures. Every message needs consent and an opt-out, and the product pricing in the message must match what the customer will be offered.
